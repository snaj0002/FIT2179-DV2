Australia Through Regional Differences

How population, opportunity, movement and climate create different regional experiences across Australia.

Overview

Australia is one country, but it is not one experience.

This project compares Australian regions through two lenses: regional socio-economic indicators from ABS data and long-term weather characteristics from Australian weather observations. The aim is not to build an expert dashboard, but to guide the reader through a clear story about where people live, where opportunity concentrates, where people move, and how climate differs across place.

How to read this story

  • Hover over charts to compare local areas, cities and states.
  • Use dropdown filters to focus on one state or city.
  • Read each section as part of one story: population, opportunity, movement and climate.

1 · Climate setting

Climate differences create the backdrop for regional life.

The map gives the environmental context before the socio-economic story begins. Each city is positioned geographically: colour represents average maximum temperature, while circle size represents rainfall. This immediately shows the contrast between the tropical north, cooler southern cities, and different coastal and inland climate patterns.

City climate markers

All observed weather locations are shown first. Use the dropdown to focus on the eight main cities. Circle size shows rainfall and colour moves from cooler blue to hotter red based on average maximum temperature.

2 · Population pattern

Population size and density tell different stories.

Big regions are not always dense regions

Population totals reveal where Australians are concentrated, highlighting the regions that place the greatest demand on housing, transport, health services and infrastructure. These are the places that dominate the national population picture.

Density tells a different story. Some regions with modest populations become extremely important once land area is considered because people live much closer together. Comparing population and density helps distinguish large regions from intensely urbanised ones and reveals how settlement patterns vary across Australia.

Largest local areas by population

The biggest local areas are shown by total residents. Colour identifies the state, using the same state palette as the density and opportunity charts.

Most densely populated local areas

Density shows where people live closest together. This changes the story from simple population size to urban compactness and land pressure.

3 · Wealth and opportunity

Opportunity is uneven, even before housing or climate are considered.

Income, education and unemployment interact

Income, education and employment are closely connected, but the relationship is not identical across Australia. Each point represents a local region, making it possible to identify both successful regions and places that do not follow broader trends.

The state-based trend lines reveal whether the relationship between opportunity indicators is stronger in some parts of Australia than others. By focusing on local regions rather than state averages, the charts expose important differences that would otherwise remain hidden.

Income vs unemployment

Higher income does not automatically remove unemployment pressure. Each coloured trend line summarises the income–unemployment relationship within a state.

Education vs income

Education and income are strongly connected in many regions, but the spread of points shows that opportunity is not evenly distributed within or between states.

State-level summary

The heatmap gives a quick state profile: which places are larger, wealthier, denser, more educated, or under more labour-market pressure relative to the others.

4 · Movement and change

Migration and unemployment trends show regions changing over time.

Movement reveals regional momentum

Migration provides a powerful indicator of regional attractiveness. Growing regions often attract workers, students and families, while declining regions may struggle to retain population and investment.

Viewed alongside unemployment trends, migration helps explain how regional economies evolve through time. These charts highlight where momentum is building, where it is slowing, and how Australia's major urban centres have changed between census periods.

Where people are moving to and from

Positive bars show regions attracting more residents than they lose; negative bars show places with net outflow. This separates growing destinations from places that may be losing residents to other parts of Australia.

Unemployment trends across major city regions

The chart focuses only on 2011, 2016 and 2021 because these are the clean comparison years in the dataset. The lines show whether labour-market pressure is rising, falling or remaining stable across selected major city regions.

Average unemployment by state

This choropleth map compares average unemployment across Australian states and territories. Darker areas indicate higher unemployment, making labour-market pressure easier to compare geographically.

5 · Climate detail

The climate layer adds another dimension to regional difference.

Climate shapes the conditions people live with

Australia spans tropical, arid, temperate and cooler southern environments. These differences influence settlement patterns, daily life, agriculture, infrastructure demand and the way regions experience risk.

The climate charts add environmental context to the population and opportunity story. Temperature, rainfall, sunshine and wind help explain why Australian regions can feel very different even when they belong to the same national economy.

Weather heatmap

The heatmap compresses many climate measures into one view, making hot, wet, dry and sunny city profiles easier to compare.

Rainfall vs temperature

Rainfall and heat together reveal different climate profiles, from hot-dry inland locations to wetter coastal cities.

Daily temperature range

The gap between minimum and maximum temperature shows how daily temperature range differs across Australian cities.

Wind profile by city

Select a city to see which wind directions dominate. Darker bars show directions recorded more often, revealing local weather patterns beyond temperature and rainfall.
